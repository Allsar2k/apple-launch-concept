# 01 · Market research

**The question:** Apple has the best spatial computer on the market and almost nobody owns one. What would a second Vision product have to be, and who would it have to be for, to turn spatial computing from a demo into a habit?

## What happened with Vision Pro

| Signal | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| Vision Pro launched in the US in February 2024 at **$3,499**. | The price put it in the "professional tool" bracket, above every laptop most students own. |
| IDC estimates about **390,000** units shipped in 2024, and about **45,000** in Q4 2025, the quarter the M5 model launched (IDC via MacRumors, 2026). | A spec bump did not move demand. The barrier is not performance. |
| Coverage of the slowdown points to **price, comfort and a thin app library** as the main reasons people pass (9to5Mac, 2026). | Three problems, and only one of them, price, is a hardware cost problem. Comfort is weight; apps are a reason-to-use problem. |
| Reports say a lighter, cheaper Vision model has been pushed back to 2027 (TweakTown, 2024). | The product this concept describes is the one the market is already waiting for. |

## Where the competition sits

| Product | Price (from) | Strength | Weakness for our audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple Vision Pro | $3,499 | Best displays, eye-and-hand input, Mac integration | Price, weight, few reasons to wear it daily |
| Meta Quest 3S | $299 | Price, gaming library | Feels like a games console; weak for work and creation |
| Meta Quest 3 | $499 | Mixed reality, games | Same positioning as 3S, heavier |
| Smart glasses (Ray-Ban Meta) | $299 | Light, social, camera | Not a computer: no screen to work on |

**The gap:** nothing combines a real computer's screen space with something light enough to wear for a two-hour study session, at a price a student can finance.

## Audience insight

- Under-25s already work across several screens at once: a laptop, a phone, often a tablet. Their problem isn't access to a screen, it's **room**: dorm desks, shared apartments, libraries.
- Creators in this age group edit, stream and post from the same small space they sleep in. A spatial display gives them a studio's worth of monitors with no studio.
- Price sensitivity is real but not absolute. Students finance phones and laptops monthly; what they need is a price that fits next to an iPhone payment, not next to rent.

## What this means for the launch

1. **Lead with room, not reality.** "Spatial computing" is Apple's category language. The audience's language is "I don't have space for a second monitor."
2. **Weight is a headline feature.** Comfort is a top reason people stop wearing headsets, so the product's lightness is a message, not a spec line.
3. **Launch with reasons to use it every day.** Pair hardware with the apps students already open: Mac Virtual Display, Final Cut Pro, Notes, FaceTime, and the creator tools they stream with.
